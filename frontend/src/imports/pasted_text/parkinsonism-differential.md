**A. Drug-Induced Parkinsonism (DIP)** — organized by mechanism and risk

- **High-risk antipsychotics (First-Gen/Typical):** haloperidol (Haldol), fluphenazine, chlorpromazine, perphenazine, thiothixene
- **Moderate-risk antipsychotics (Second-Gen/Atypical):** risperidone (Risperdal), paliperidone (Invega), quetiapine (Seroquel), olanzapine (Zyprexa), aripiprazole (Abilify), ziprasidone (Geodon), clozapine (Clozaril)
- **Other psychiatric meds (tremor risk):** lithium, valproate/valproic acid (Depakote)
- **Antiemetics/prokinetics — central dopamine antagonists:** metoclopramide, prochlorperazine, promethazine
- **Antiemetics/prokinetics — peripheral dopamine antagonists:** domperidone, levosulpiride
- **Dopamine depleters (VMAT2 inhibitors):** tetrabenazine, deutetrabenazine, valbenazine (Ingrezza)
- **Blood pressure meds:** reserpine, methyldopa
- **Calcium channel blockers (also used for migraine/vertigo):** flunarizine, cinnarizine
- **Migraine/vertigo meds:** metoclopramide (Reglan), prochlorperazine (Compazine), promethazine (Phenergan), dimenhydrinate (Dramamine)

**B. Non-medical / toxic exposures**

- **Illicit drugs:** cocaine, amphetamines, opioids
- **Synthetic contaminants; alcohol withdrawal**
- **Pesticides/herbicides:** paraquat
- **Insecticides:** rotenone
- **Heavy metals/solvents:** manganese (PD-like gait + stiffness), lead, mercury, trichloroethylene
- **Severe Vitamin B6 deficiency context:** chronic alcohol use disorder, high-risk drugs, GI/malabsorption (inflammatory bowel disease, bariatric surgery, chronic kidney disease), autoimmune disorders (rheumatoid arthritis, SLE), genetic disorders

**C. Falls & orthostatic history**

- Recurrent, unprovoked falls (often backward) within the first year → suggests PSP, not PD
- Orthostatic hypotension: drop of >30 mm Hg systolic or >15 mm Hg diastolic early in disease (red flag for MSA/autonomic disorders)

**D. Autonomic & non-motor history** (also rule out diabetes as a cause)

- **Urogenital:** unexplained urinary retention, severe incontinence, or early severe erectile dysfunction within the first 5 years; severe urinary failure within 1–2 years → major MSA red flag
- **Constipation:** often the first symptom, appearing years/decades before tremor (also seen in other diseases)
- **Neurogenic orthostatic hypotension (nOH):** in PD usually mid-to-late and manageable; severe early drops with frequent fainting → MSA red flag

**E. Cerebrovascular history (Vascular Parkinsonism)**

- Idiopathic PD has no direct link to stroke history; it's slow and gradual
- Discard PD if: sudden/stepwise onset after a vascular event, or strong vascular risk factors (poorly controlled hypertension, diabetes, prior strokes)

**F. PD-supportive features to record as positives**

- RBD (acting out dreams, thrashing, vocalizing) — highly specific to synucleinopathies
- Olfactory dysfunction not due to sinus disease, COVID-19, or smoking
- Chronic, long-standing constipation predating motor symptoms

## 3) Symptoms & examination module (clinic visit)

**A. Symmetry (compare left vs. right across motor signs, gait, automatic movements, posture)**

- Perfectly symmetric onset from day one = Absolute Exclusion Criterion for idiopathic PD (MDS guidelines)
- Evaluation: compare the left and right sides across four major domains: **motor signs, functional gait, automatic movements, and posture.**
- PD almost universally presents asymmetrically:
    - **Resting tremor**
        - Unilateral Hand Shake: tremor that occurs when the limb is fully relaxed and supported. It typically starts in one hand or foot while the opposing limb remains completely still.
        - **Asymmetrical Re-emergence - single arm tremor or rising:** Ask the patient to hold both arms straight out in front of them. If a tremor is present, it will typically re-emerge on the initially affected side first after a few seconds of holding the posture.
    - **Rigidity & posture:**
        - **Resistance to movement of body parts - Lead-Pipe or Cogwheel Rigidity:** When you passively flex and extend the patient's wrists and elbows, you will feel a distinct, asymmetrical resistance ("cogwheeling") on one side of the body compared to the other.
        - **Asymmetrical Postural Leaning:** Early in the disease, a patient may show a subtle lateral tilt of the trunk or a slight drooping of one shoulder on the affected side when standing or walking.
    - **Bradykinesia Slowness and Decreased Amplitude):**
        - Finger Taps slowness: Have the patient tap their index finger and thumb together as fast and as wide as possible. Look for an asymmetrical reduction in speed or a progressive "cloning"
        - **unilateral Hand Movements slowness :** Observe for asymmetry as the patient opens and closes their fists rapidly. One hand will often show a noticeable lag or hesitation.
        - **Toe Taps / Foot Agile:** Observe the patient tapping their heel or toe on the floor; look for asymmetry in the height or speed of the taps.
        - **Asymmetrical Micrographia:** While writing a sentence, the patient's dominant hand may show progressively smaller and cramped handwriting, which is a localized manifestation of unilateral bradykinesia.
    - **Gait & automatic movements:**
        - **Reduced Arm Swing:** This is often the earliest identifiable sign of asymmetry. When walking, the patient will naturally swing one arm normally, while the affected arm will hang loosely, stay rigid, or remain pinned slightly flexed against the torso.
        - **Asymmetrical Foot Drag:** Look for a subtle "shuffling" or trailing foot on the affected side, where the patient drags their toes or fails to pick the foot up completely during the stride.

**B. Gait red flags (non-PD)**

- **VP "lower-body" Parkinsonism:** severe leg shuffling/stiffness but normal arm swing
- **NPH "magnetic" gait:** feet "glued" to floor + urinary incontinence + memory issues (NPH triad)
- **VP wide-based gait:** feet wide apart (PD walks with narrow stance)

**C. Tremor classification**

- Tremor while in action (kinetic) may not be PD → consider Essential Tremor or Dystonic Tremor
